# Phase 5 — User Experience Design

## The design thesis

Every competitor puts numbers first and makes you interpret them. This product
puts **the conclusion first** and lets you drill down into the evidence. Every
screen follows the same information hierarchy:

**Verdict → Reasons → Evidence → Raw data**

If a user only reads the first line of any screen, they should still have
learned something true and useful.

Visual direction: dark, dense, fast, high-contrast. The audience is gamers who
live in dark UIs. Avoid the "enterprise SaaS dashboard" look — this should feel
closer to a good esports broadcast graphic than to Salesforce. Restraint on
color: team colors (CT blue / T yellow) carry meaning, so decorative color must
be minimal or the meaningful color stops reading.

---

## Landing page

**Job:** convince a skeptical player in 8 seconds that this is different from
every stats site they've already tried.

- **Hero:** not a feature list. A real report excerpt, verbatim, with the
  citation links visible. Headline in the product's own voice: *"Stop reading
  stats. Start understanding losses."* Sub: *"Upload a demo. Get told exactly
  why you lost and what to fix."* Single CTA: **Analyze a demo — free**.
- **The comparison moment:** side-by-side. Left, a wall of numbers labeled
  "What every other site gives you." Right, three sentences of specific
  coaching. This does more selling than any feature grid.
- **How it works:** three steps, illustrated, 15 words each.
- **Live sample report** — a real, public, fully interactive report from a real
  demo, no signup. Let people touch the product before the wall. This is the
  highest-converting element on the page and worth building properly.
- **Round viewer teaser** — an autoplaying loop of the 2D replay. Motion sells
  the technical credibility.
- **Social proof:** rank-labeled quotes ("Premier 19k"). Rank labels are the
  credibility currency here, not job titles.
- **Pricing**, **FAQ** (Is my data safe? Does this work with FACEIT? Is this
  cheating? — that last one matters and must be answered plainly: post-match
  analysis of your own demos, no game interaction, VAC-irrelevant).
- Footer with Discord, privacy, terms.

Performance target: LCP under 1.5s. Static, no client JS above the fold.

## Dashboard (`/app`)

The home surface after login. Job: get you to your latest insight in one click.

- **Top strip:** "Since your last 5 matches" — 3 headline movements (one
  improving, one declining, one streak), each a sentence, not a number.
- **Primary card:** most recent match — map thumbnail, score, your rating, and
  the **top finding as a sentence**. Click goes to the report, not a stats page.
- **Match list:** compact rows — map, date, score with W/L color, your rating,
  a one-line insight summary, status pill for anything still processing.
  Filters: map, result, mode, date range. Infinite scroll.
- **Persistent upload affordance** — a dropzone in the header that accepts a
  file drop anywhere on the page.
- **Empty state is a first-run experience, not an apology.** "Drop your first
  demo here" with a link to *how to find your demo files* (real friction: most
  players don't know where `.dem` files live or how to get one from Premier).
  Plus a **"See a sample report instead"** button so a user with no demo handy
  still experiences the product.

## Upload page (`/app/upload`)

- Full-page dropzone, also accepts paste and file picker. Multi-file queue.
- Per-file: name, size, progress, speed, ETA, cancel. Resume on refresh.
- Hash-and-check happens during upload; a dedupe hit short-circuits to "Already
  analyzed — view report."
- **Inline help that actually helps:** where demos live
  (`Steam/steamapps/common/Counter-Strike Global Offensive/game/csgo/`), how to
  download a Premier demo from the in-game watch tab, and the difference between
  a POV and a GOTV demo — with a note that POV demos won't work and why.
- Later (M10): "Connect Steam for automatic analysis" as the escape from ever
  visiting this page again.
- Errors are specific and actionable, never "upload failed."

## Processing screen (`/app/demos/:id/processing`)

Ninety seconds of waiting is where users leave. Design it as content, not a
spinner.

- **Real staged progress** driven by SSE: `Uploading → Reading demo →
  Reconstructing 24 rounds → Analyzing your play → Writing your report`, with
  per-stage completion and a live count ("Round 18 of 24").
- **Progressive reveal:** the moment parsing completes, show the scoreboard and
  timeline *while* the AI is still writing. The user is reading real data within
  ~40 seconds instead of staring at a bar.
- The report then **streams in section by section** underneath. Watching it
  write is itself engaging and communicates that real work is happening.
- Failure state: plain-language cause, what to do, retry button, contact link.
- Notification opt-in ("email me when it's done") for slow paths.

## Match report (`/app/matches/:id`)

The core screen. Vertical narrative, drill-down on demand.

1. **Verdict block** — the headline. *"You lost this on the CT side: 3 of 4
   B-site retakes failed because utility was already spent."* One or two
   sentences, largest text on the page.
2. **Match header** — map, score, half split, date, mode, your rating.
3. **Your top 3 mistakes** — expandable cards. Collapsed: the claim plus the
   number. Expanded: explanation, the supporting metric with its percentile and
   sample size, cited rounds as clickable chips that open the round viewer at
   the exact tick, and a concrete drill.
4. **What you did well** — one card. Non-negotiable. A product that only
   criticizes gets abandoned regardless of accuracy.
5. **Turning-point rounds** — 3–5 rounds with a narrative and a "watch" link.
6. **Team read** — economy, execute success, trade discipline, site
   distribution.
7. **Round timeline** — the full strip, win/loss, end reason, economy, expand
   for kill feed.
8. **Full scoreboard** — collapsed by default. It exists for trust, not for
   coaching; anyone who wants it will look for it.
9. **Ask about this match** — persistent chat entry, prefilled with three
   suggested questions derived from the actual findings.
10. **Feedback** — thumbs on every AI section. Small, always visible.
11. **Share** — public link with an auto-generated OG image showing the verdict.

Tabs across the top for teammates so a 5-stack navigates one report, not five.

## Round viewer (`/app/matches/:id/rounds/:n`)

- Radar canvas centered, timeline scrubber beneath, event markers on the track.
- Player dots with team color, view cones, name labels, HP rings; dimmed on
  death. Grenade trajectories, smoke circles with real radius, molotov
  footprints, bomb icon and plant timer.
- Controls: space to play/pause, arrow keys to step, `[`/`]` for events, speed
  0.25×–4×, round selector.
- **Left rail:** the round's event log, clickable to seek.
- **Right rail:** the findings that cite *this* round, so evidence and claim sit
  on one screen. This is the trust-building moment of the whole product.
- Deep-linkable to `?tick=`. Every AI citation is one of these links.

## Player profile (`/app/players/:steamId`)

- Identity header: avatar, rank, matches analyzed, inferred role with the
  behavioral reason for the inference.
- **Trajectory chart** — rating over time with match markers.
- **Strengths and weaknesses** — two columns, plain sentences with percentiles.
- **Recurring mistakes** — the streak tracker. Each with occurrence count,
  trend arrow, and status (active / improving / resolved). Resolved items stay
  visible as achievements; deleting them removes the proof of progress, which is
  the whole point.
- **Map breakdown** — per-map W/L, rating, and the single biggest per-map issue.
- **Heatmaps** — deaths, kills, and utility origins, toggleable by side.
- **Progress report** — the periodic LLM longitudinal summary.

## AI chat (`/app/chat`, and embedded per match)

- Standard streaming chat, but with product-specific affordances:
  - **Tool-call transparency** — a collapsed "Looked at 24 rounds, 8 opening
    duels" line above the answer. Showing the work is what separates this from
    a chatbot guessing.
  - **Inline citations** render as round chips; hovering previews the radar,
    clicking opens the viewer.
  - **Data blocks** — when the answer involves comparison, the model returns a
    small table or chart component, not a wall of prose numbers.
  - **Scope selector** — this match / last 10 matches / all time.
  - **Suggested questions** generated from the user's actual findings, not
    generic prompts.
- Conversation history in a sidebar, auto-titled.
- Rate-limit and quota states are informative, not punitive.

## Settings (`/app/settings`)

Tabs: **Account** (Steam link, email, display name, delete account with a real
export-first flow) · **Connections** (Steam auto-ingest, FACEIT) ·
**Preferences** (coaching tone: direct/balanced/encouraging — a genuinely
differentiating setting; primary role; notification prefs; units) ·
**Billing** (plan, usage vs. quota shown as a bar, invoices, portal) ·
**Privacy** (public sharing defaults, teammate name anonymization, data export).

## Mobile experience

Assume a meaningful share of report reading happens on a phone right after a
session ends. Mobile is a **reading** experience; the viewer is desktop-first.

- Reports: single column, full fidelity, collapsible sections. This works well
  on mobile and should be excellent.
- Chat: excellent on mobile, full-screen, native-feeling.
- Dashboard: card list.
- **Round viewer:** functional but reduced — pinch-zoom radar, simplified
  controls, no keyboard shortcuts. Show a "better on desktop" hint rather than
  pretending parity.
- **Upload on mobile:** deliberately deprioritized — demos live on a PC. Instead
  offer "send yourself a link" and push auto-ingest, which is the correct mobile
  answer.
- Tables become stacked key-value cards. Never horizontal-scroll a scoreboard.
- Bottom tab bar: Home · Matches · Chat · Profile.

## Cross-cutting UX rules

- **Loading:** skeletons matching final layout, never spinners on primary
  content. Any wait over 3 seconds gets staged progress.
- **Errors:** cause + fix + action, every time.
- **Empty states:** always offer the sample-data path.
- **Accessibility:** keyboard navigation throughout, WCAG AA contrast (a dark
  gaming palette fails this by default — check it), radar viewer needs a text
  event log as its accessible equivalent, no color-only encoding.
- **Speed:** report pages are static content and should feel instant. Target
  sub-1s on repeat views.
